# research

Notes and source material feeding the concept track. Organised by theme; each topic gets its own file when there's enough to draft. The centre of gravity is now **attention as a commons** (see [docs/development-log.md](../docs/development-log.md) for how the framing got there); the older commons/mutualism and metacrisis threads sit underneath it.

## Attention as a commons — the core

- **The enclosure claim** — attention as a shared resource being enclosed by platforms, advertising, and algorithmic feeds; the "attention economy" extracts what should be held in common. Reference points: **James Williams** (*Stand Out of Our Light*), **Matthew Crawford** (*The World Beyond Your Head*), the **Center for Humane Technology** (Tristan Harris). Historical analogy: the English land *enclosures*; a Marxist alienation reading also fits (alienation from the depth of one's own attention).
- **The bounded-commons problem** — classical commons are local and bounded (Ostrom); attention is universal. The unresolved-then-resolved tension is the central conceptual move of the project.
  - *Resolution A* — contemplative communities (sangha, retreat centre, practice group) **are** the commons: bounded, with shared norms and mutual accountability, tending not land but the *conditions* for attention (silence, practice, relational norms, transmitted knowledge). Lineage transmission as commons governance — maps onto Ostrom's criteria.
  - *Resolution B* — **Contemplative Commons** as a knowledge commons: the *wisdom about attention* (practices, maps, discoveries across traditions) held openly, like open source for inner development.
  - *Layered framing* — commons at the cultural/institutional level (governable) vs. attention-as-*ground/birthright* at the existential level (inexhaustible, only recognisable). "Commons can be governed. Ground can only be recognised."
- **Monitoring commons as collective interoception** — a group's capacity to notice the quality of its own shared attention and course-correct before drift becomes capture (not surveillance).
- **The AI angle** — "Attention Is All You Need" ([arXiv:1706.03762](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)) as a touchstone; "Homo Agenticus" (Rohit Krishnan) and the idea of an *agentic commons* — proliferating AI agents each demanding a share of the same finite attention.

## Commons and mutualism

- **Elinor Ostrom** — *Governing the Commons* (1990); Nobel 2009 for demonstrating commons as a viable third way beyond market and state. Her **eight design principles for long-enduring commons** are the thing to re-read and translate into an attention-culture blueprint.
- **David Bollier** — *Free, Fair and Alive*; *Patterns of Commoning*; *Think Like a Commoner* (2nd ed. released into open access). Commoning as a *social practice* and a process of subjectivation, not just a resource type. A [Think Like a Commoner MCP server](https://github.com/zhiganov/think-like-a-commoner-mcp) (built by Artem) makes the book usable in conversation with Claude.
- **Michel Bauwens / P2P Foundation** — [Four Future P2P Scenarios](https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Four_Future_P2P_Scenarios), [Resilient Communities](https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Resilient_Communities); transnational "phyles" (alliances of ethical enterprises around a knowledge commons). Wikipedia as a rare example of a successful *global* commons that balances centralised infrastructure with a non-profit focus on the shared resource.
- **Pierre-Joseph Proudhon** — *What is Property?* (1840); mutualism as a 19th-century precursor of commons thinking.
- **Peter Kropotkin** — *Mutual Aid: A Factor of Evolution* (1902).
- **Max Stirner**, **the 19th-century anarchist cluster** — as "advanced governance thinkers" (Artem): the people who thought hardest about coordination without coercion.
- **Kassa vzaimopomoshchi / mutual aid fund** — concrete 19th-century mechanism; open question why none exists for Russian-speaking emigration.

## Metacrisis — as contested frame

The repo treats metacrisis as a hypothesis to probe, not as established fact.

- **Daniel Schmachtenberger** — Lex Fridman interview (2020) as entry point; popularised "massive coordination failure."
- **Scott Alexander** — *Meditations on Moloch* (2014); multi-agent rationality producing civilisational loss.
- **Empirical critics** — Alexey Kapterev's challenge: which specific parameters are objectively worse than before? Apophenia as a risk of the frame.
- The question of whether the frame can be measured prospectively vs only retrospectively.

## Principle-based organisations

- **Alcoholics Anonymous** — the 12 Steps; global reach from one book; durably decentralised; appeal to a "higher power" as structural (not dogmatic). Flagged as the strongest contemporary exemplar.
- **Russian Old Believers** — post-raskol forced decentralisation → mutual-aid funds → work ethic → industrial capitalisation → financing of serf emancipation.
- **Order of Interbeing** (Plum Village) — 14 mindfulness trainings as principles; sub-organisations (ecological, social) federated under shared principles but otherwise uncoupled.
- **Sōka Gakkai** — lay Buddhist global network; reference with reservations (sectarian tendencies but real longevity).

## Belief systems as governance

- Religions and ideologies as governance patterns (what gets enforced, what gets rewarded, what the shared story is).
- Old Believers as case study of belief → economic structure → social outcome.
- The principle-governance parallel: AA ≈ lay sangha ≈ NGO constitution ≈ civic order.
- Artem's thesis: loss of principled governance at state level is one of the strongest metacrisis indicators worth arguing for.

## Neurophenomenology / embodied mind

- **Francisco Varela + Evan Thompson + Eleanor Rosch** — *The Embodied Mind* (1991).
- **Evan Thompson** — continuing the Varela programme; *Mind in Life*, *Waking, Dreaming, Being*; philosophical Buddhism meeting cognitive science.
- **William Irwin Thompson** — Evan's father; Lindisfarne Association (Canada, 1970s–80s); cross-tradition intellectual crucible (Tibetan Buddhists, Fritz Perls, etc. all in one room).

## Hemispheres — why the frame resists measurement

- **Iain McGilchrist** — *The Master and His Emissary*, *The Matter with Things*. Working hypothesis: "metacrisis" is inherently right-hemispheric in shape (pattern, relation, meaning) and therefore resists left-hemispheric demand for discrete measurable parameters — a useful lens on the Kapterev critique.

## Japanese Buddhist utopian communities

Early-20th-century quasi-Buddhist communities — not monastic, not sect-bound, but principled and practice-based. These are a structural reference for the "Sōka Gakkai in a new key" form.

- ***A Buddha Land in This World: Philosophy, Utopia, and Radical Buddhism*** — open-access monograph on JSTOR ([j.ctv2gz3zt3](https://www.jstor.org/content/oa_book_monograph/j.ctv2gz3zt3)). Shared by Viktor 2026-04-20 as the reference he had partly read.

## Facilitation patterns

- **Climate Fresk** — open-source climate workshop; anyone can download and print the materials; free sessions run by certified facilitators worldwide; 2–3 hours of collaborative card-laying around a table. Reference for the *kit* form.
- **Pandemic** (board game, 2008) — cooperative-only; "no one can win alone, only everyone can lose" as pedagogy; historically the top-ranked game on BoardGameGeek.
- **Mindfulness card decks** — Vishvapani's 2008 deck referenced as a rare example of stackable cards *with rules*, not passive prompts. Most later decks lost the game layer.

## Strategy, futures, and frameworks

- **Theory of change** — informed by a Royal Society *Interface* paper on civilizational burnout / homeostasis ([rsif.2022.0029](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rsif.2022.0029)). A "Three Moves" framing; first move: *establish and maintain the conditions in which this quality of attention can be cultivated collectively, not only individually.*
- **Three Horizons (Bill Sharpe)** — H1 managerial (maintain), H2 entrepreneurial (grasp opportunities), H3 visionary (act at odds with current values). Structure for the theory of change.
- **The four-quadrant strategic map (Viktor)** — integral quadrants × everyday/deep. Densely covered: everyday individual phenomenology (mindfulness-as-product) and everyday attention science. The three "pink zones" (voids) are the project's territory: collective·exterior·everyday (no stewardship between wellness market and isolated monasteries — Bollier's ground), collective·interior·everyday (no shared language for attentiveness in families/teams/cities), individual·exterior·deep (science of deep states only emerging). Implication: *not* another meditation app or mindfulness school; rather stewardship + everyday collective-attention culture + a "continuous vertical" linking surface to depth. *(Map images were photos, not in the conversation export — described from Viktor's text.)*
- **Mikhail Bakhtin** — architectonics of the act (*I-for-myself, the-other-for-me, I-for-the-other*) as a relational frame for attention.
- **Adjacent futures reading** — ["We May Be Entering a Second Axial Age"](https://www.noemamag.com/we-may-be-entering-a-second-axial-age) (Noema); ["Toward Wayfinding Infrastructure for the Living Web"](https://www.atlasresear.ch/blog/toward-wayfinding-infrastructure-for-the-living-web/) (Atlas Research Group); The Protocol Institute / *Summer of Protocols* (Venkatesh Rao) — on the radar, current tracks not yet a fit.

## Contemplative science of deep states

- **Richard Davidson, Antoine Lutz** — pioneers of the neuroscience of advanced contemplative states; flagged as a field that exists but is *not yet* a discipline with reproducible protocols (the "individual·exterior·deep" void on the map).
