# Broad listening — Plurality's augmentation answer to attention scarcity

Notes on how *Plurality* treats attention, read against this project's attention-as-commons frame. Plurality shares the enclosure diagnosis almost exactly, then proposes a response the project hasn't centred: not withdrawal, and not only defence, but **augmentation** — using AI to let a group digest more diversity than unaided attention can hold. Worth holding as a live option *and* a live question, not a settled answer.

**Source.** Artem built a Plurality MCP (sibling to the Think Like a Commoner MCP referenced elsewhere in this repo) embedding *Plurality: The Future of Collaborative Technology and Democracy* — E. Glen Weyl, Audrey Tang & ⿻ Community (2024, **CC0 / public domain**). The passages below are verbatim from that catalogue, with the term / passage IDs noted; the book imposes no license restriction. ## Shared diagnosis: information abundant, attention scarce

Plurality starts where the enclosure claim starts. Its glossary names the regime directly:

> **attention economy** — "The regime of digital communication in which information is abundant and human attention is the scarce resource. Plurality argues that this environment fundamentally alters the meaning of free speech: adversaries seeking to suppress inconvenient views find it more effective to flood the information commons with distractions and spam than to censor directly… The challenge becomes surfacing diverse, relevant, and genuine content — not only preventing literal censorship."

The inversion the project draws from Williams and Crawford, Plurality states in the same terms *(`free-speech-information-abundance-challenge`)*:

> "…This assumption was founded on an environment where information was scarce and thus its suppression was one of the more effective ways to avoid voices being heard. The present environment is different: information is abundant and attention scarce."

And the mechanism this repo calls *enclosure* — Artem's "Facebook and Twitter pump attention out of people like oil, a classic Moloch, since the polarisation is bad even for shareholders" — Plurality names as a failure mode *(`media-engagement-maximization-failure`)*:

> "The dominant model of digital media—advertising-supported, attention-maximizing platforms—creates systematic misalignment between commercial incentives and social value. Such platforms profit most when they maximize engagement, which research consistently shows means amplifying outrage, tribalism, and misinformation."

Same claim, supplied with citation and failure-mode vocabulary. Plurality even reaches for "the information commons" unprompted. The diagnosis is not in dispute between the two frames.

## The augmentation move

Where this project's two resolutions *defend or cultivate* attention (contemplative communities as the commons; wisdom-about-attention as a knowledge commons), Plurality asks a different question: once attention is scarce, what do you do *with* it at the scale of a public? Its answer is broad listening:

> **broad listening** — "The capacity for one person or system to thoughtfully digest a wide range of perspectives at scale — as distinct from 'broadcast' (allowing many to hear a single statement). As Herbert Simon's observation implies, a wealth of information creates a poverty of attention, making broad listening extremely costly and time-consuming."

The central passage states the constraint and the cost *(`broad-listening-attention-poverty`, Ch. 5-4)*:

> "…while methods of *broadcast* (allowing many to hear a single statement) have dramatically improved, *broad listening* (allowing one person to thoughtfully digest a range of perspectives) remains extremely costly and time consuming. As economics Nobel Laureate and computer science pioneer Herbert Simon observed, '(A) wealth of information creates a poverty of attention.' The cognitive limits on the amount of attention an individual can give… potentially impose sharp trade-offs between diversity and bandwidth, as well as between richness and inclusion."

Two tradeoffs to keep: **diversity ↔ bandwidth** (hear more voices, or hear each more deeply) and **richness ↔ inclusion** (deep engagement with a few, or shallow reach to many). And note the wording: "one person *or system*." The listener can be infrastructure, not only an individual — which is what makes it a *commons* question rather than a self-help one.

## The cluster: infrastructure for collective attention

Broad listening is a primitive under **augmented deliberation** (Ch. 5-4); the neighbours that make it work map onto this project's vocabulary more than incidentally.

> **augmented deliberation** — "The use of digital tools — AI-assisted synthesis, visual mapping, machine translation, immersive environments — to deepen and scale deliberative processes… does not replace human judgment but amplifies collective sense-making: surfacing areas of hidden consensus… without collapsing disagreement into false consensus."

> **collective response systems** (Ovadya & Thorburn) — "…combine the participatory, open, and interactive nature of social media with features that encourage thoughtful listening… and the careful emergence of shared views and points of rough consensus. Examples include Polis, All Our Ideas, and Remesh. Distinguished from simple polls or comment threads by their capacity to surface bridging statements that receive assent across otherwise-dividing lines."

> **bridging systems** — "…prioritizing content… that receive support across otherwise-divided groups. Used in Community Notes' 'bridging-based ranking,' in Polis's surfacing of cross-cluster consensus statements… The defining principle is rewarding cross-group assent rather than within-group popularity."

Two of these touch the project directly:

- **Collective response systems ≈ collective interoception.** This repo's "monitoring commons" is a group's capacity to notice the quality of its own shared attention and course-correct before drift becomes capture. A collective response system is close to interoception *tooling* for a public — it lets a group perceive the structure of its own shared view (clusters, divides, rough consensus) rather than only its average.
- **Bridging ≈ Ostromian governance.** Rewarding cross-group assent over within-group popularity is a digital instance of the rough-consensus-over-majority-capture logic the project wants to translate from Ostrom's eight principles.

## Where it lands on the four-quadrant map

Broad listening is a built, running example of "commons governance rather than withdrawal" — Viktor's map locates it cleanly in the **collective · exterior · everyday** pink zone (the void with no stewardship between the wellness market and the isolated monastery; Bollier's territory). It is governance and infrastructure for shared attention.

It is also worth saying what it is *not*. It touches none of the interior verticals and nothing of attention-as-ground (the luminosity "that can only be recognised, not managed"). It is exterior-collective plumbing. It fills one pink zone and leaves the others untouched — which is exactly why it reads as *necessary, not sufficient* for a theory of change that wants less-capturable agents.

## The AI angle, against the agentic-commons worry

The project's AI thread runs pessimistic: "Attention Is All You Need" as touchstone, then *Homo Agenticus* — agents proliferate, each consuming the same finite attention, so even a ban on social media would leave the commons getting drawn down, now by agents. Broad listening is the constructive counterpoint: AI as *listening infrastructure that helps a collective attend*, rather than one more claimant on attention. Same substrate — generative foundation models are built on the transformer attention mechanism — opposite valence. The resonance (the machine's "attention" turned back against the human poverty of attention) is real enough to note, though it stays a pun until the governance question below is answered.

## Hold as hypothesis

Per this repo's discipline — keep the civilizational frame and the practical questions distinguishable, and don't let optimism answer the hard questions — Plurality's claim is the part to probe, not adopt. The book asserts that GFMs and collective response systems "address this bottleneck, enabling augmented deliberation at previously unimaginable scales," breaking the diversity and richness tradeoffs. Open questions worth carrying:

- **Who governs the synthesis model?** If a model surfaces the "rough consensus," is that commoning — or a fresh enclosure of the *sensemaking function itself*, with the model as landlord of meaning? A commons whose synthesis layer is privately owned may not be a commons.
- **Does it reduce the attention load or add to it?** Another mediation layer, another agent in the loop — the agentic-commons problem in new clothes. That it nets out positive is an empirical bet, not a given.
- **Exterior only.** It governs the shared resource; it does not cultivate the interior conditions the project treats as the deeper move. Broad listening is plausibly load-bearing infrastructure for the collective-exterior void, and silent everywhere else.

Provisional, like the rest of the frame: a strong candidate instrument for one quadrant, and a clean test case for whether attention can be stewarded as a commons by augmentation rather than withdrawal.
