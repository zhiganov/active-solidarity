### The reframe

Viktor retired his three-year project *Active Presence (Деятельное Присутствие)* and rebranded it Culture of Attention, marking the transition with a community ceremony on **2026-05-10**. The umbrella also holds his educational programs and a Buddhist track he calls *Marvelous Gates (Чудесные врата)*.
- **Attention Commons** is the commons-and-governance arm — the part Artem and Viktor are thinking through together.

- Domains acquired: `attentioncommons.org`, `cultureofattention.org`.

### Attention as a commons — the core argument

- **The enclosure claim.** Attention is a shared resource being enclosed by platforms, advertising and algorithmic feeds — "attention economy" extracts what should be held in common. Reference points: James Williams (*Stand Out of Our Light*), Matthew Crawford, the Center for Humane Technology. Artem's framing: Facebook and Twitter pump attention out of people like oil — a classic Moloch, since the polarisation it produces is bad even for the shareholders.
- **The tension.** Classical commons are always specific and bounded (this meadow, this well); Ostrom's point is that successful commons are *local*. Attention is universal. So "Attention Commons" as a global concept has a real conceptual problem.
- **Resolution A — contemplative communities *are* the commons.** A sangha, a retreat centre, a practice group: a bounded community with shared norms, mutual accountability, and a shared resource it tends together — not land, but the *conditions* that support attention (silence, shared practice, relational norms, transmitted knowledge). Teacher–student lineage as a form of commons governance. This maps onto Ostrom's criteria precisely.
- **Resolution B — Contemplative Commons as a knowledge commons.** The resource held in common is the *wisdom about attention* — practices, maps, discoveries accumulated across traditions. Closer to a knowledge/cultural commons; "open-source software, but for inner development." The Creative Commons analogy: practices held openly, transmissible freely, not enclosed by any single tradition. **CC BY-SA for practices** is floated as a real, workable proposal.
- **The layered framing.** Attention-as-commons at the cultural and institutional level (practices, knowledge, conditions — these can be *governed*) and attention-as-*birthright/ground* at the existential level (the "luminosity," inexhaustible, never enclosed even when inaccessible — this can only be *recognised*, not managed). "Commons can be governed. Ground can only be recognised."
- **Ostrom.** Her eight design principles for successful commons are taken as something to re-read in the attention-culture domain.

### Civilizational frame vs. practical questions

- **Moloch and capture.** The reason Moloch keeps winning isn't only bad incentives — the agents inside those structures are themselves captured: fragmented attention, dissociated from consequence, unable to hold long horizons. The hypothesis: practices cultivating sustained, embodied, wide-aperture awareness produce agents who are structurally *less capturable*. "Historically this was achieved through withdrawal. Our hypothesis is that it can be achieved through commons governance rather than withdrawal — which makes it a civilizational intervention rather than a personal practice."
- **Monitoring commons** = not surveillance, but *collective interoception* — a group's developed capacity to notice the quality of its own shared attention and course-correct before drift becomes capture.
- **The discipline (Viktor's "Claude," and Viktor endorsing it).** Keep the civilizational frame and the practical questions distinguishable. The practical questions are stronger and more honest, and they are *genuinely Ostromian* when posed at the level of a sangha or teaching body: *What does this practice do to a life? To a community of lives? Can attention practice resist the commodification that captured nearly every other contemplative form imported into the West? What governance preserves the substance rather than letting it dissolve into wellness content?* The risk is using the civilizational frame to *answer* the practical questions ("we should do X because civilization depends on it") rather than letting the practical questions inform the frame. Artem's counter: without the civilizational frame it's hard to answer "why at all" — a theory of change needs both.

### Frameworks and artefacts produced

- **Theory of Change.** Drafted with Claude (artifacts exist), informed by a Royal Society *Interface* paper on civilizational burnout / homeostasis ([rsif.2022.0029](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rsif.2022.0029)) — "collective self-regulation, civilizational homeostasis." A "Three Moves" framing emerged; the first move: *establish and maintain the conditions in which this quality of attention can be cultivated collectively, not only individually.*
- **Three Horizons (Bill Sharpe).** H1 managerial (maintain the system), H2 entrepreneurial (grasp its opportunities), H3 visionary/aspirational (make something happen even at odds with current values). Used as a structure for the theory of change.
- **Blueprints / templates.** A recurring idea: produce reusable templates different communities can pick up — Ostrom's principles translated into an attention-culture blueprint.
- **The four-quadrant strategic map (Viktor).** An integral-style map (individual/collective × interior/exterior; everyday vs. deep). What it makes visible: the densely-covered zones are everyday individual phenomenology (mindfulness-as-product) and everyday attention science — where the money and grants already are. The interesting work is in the three "pink zones" (voids): (1) *collective · exterior · everyday* — no common stewardship between the wellness market and isolated monasteries (exactly Bollier's territory); (2) *collective · interior · everyday* — no common culture or language for attentiveness in families, teams, cities (everyone reinvents the wheel); (3) *individual · exterior · deep* — the science of deep states is only emerging (Davidson, Lutz as pioneers, not yet a reproducible-protocol discipline). Conclusion: Attention Commons is *not* "another meditation app" or "another mindfulness school" (those zones are saturated) — it is stewardship, everyday collective-attention culture, and connection to deep science. Active Presence + Marvelous Gates read, retrospectively, as instruments tuned to stitch the deep traditions to transmission formats to everyday focus — building a *continuous vertical* in each quadrant so a person can ascend gradually without being stuck at the surface or forced into a monastery for depth.

### The manifesto (Viktor's, in progress)

Viktor drafted a manifesto for **Culture of Attention** — written on a plane, originally for his **book** (intended for the introduction and the chapter-8 conclusion). Its moves:

- Culture of attention *exists*: rooted in biology and evolution (every conscious being has ways of attending), bodily in basis, reaching all the way to contemplative traditions that use attention for ultimate liberation — and everything in between (the attention economy, the distraction industry, mundane concentration, falling asleep at the wheel).
- One field, explored at its different edges across the whole of civilization: Ptahhotep, shamans, Socrates and Plato, the Buddha, Hume and Kant, modern neuroscientists — all manifestations of one culture of attention.
- We have the term "attention economy" but no "culture of attention" — the thing exists but the word doesn't. The invitation is to see the field *as a whole*, and to see that the fight for our attention and the practice of liberating attention are two ends of the same field.
- Without a culture of attention, the other useful cultures (hygiene, physical activity, mental health, civic activity) are under threat and don't work — each competes for attention with what destroys it.
- The proposal: make the culture of attention *visible*, as we once made hygiene and mental health visible — not an elite practice, not a consumer product, but a commons; study the artefacts of contemplative traditions and how they are governed; free attention as a public resource from those who use it against us.

Viktor flagged the Claude-polished version as "a bit sweet and partly off the mark," and it is his book material. It is represented here by its key moves only; the finished text is his to publish.

### Enclosure and alienation

- Facebook et al. enclose *fields of attention* the way English landlords once enclosed common fields (the historical *enclosures*). A Marxist-alienation reading also fits: alienating people from the depth of their own attention is doing something inherently bad, as alienating workers from the products of their labour was.
- Viktor's caveat: for now this is still largely a zone of personal responsibility — no one yet *forces* you to scroll (though ad-bearing fridge interfaces are a literal step toward invasion). The companies' unethical behaviour is clear; people also engage themselves.

### The AI / agentic-attention angle

- The transformer paper "Attention Is All You Need" ([arXiv:1706.03762](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)) is a recurring touchstone — attention is also a core AI concept.
- "Homo Agenticus" (Rohit Krishnan, Strange Loop Canon) prompted a new line: as AI agents proliferate, each one demands attention. Even if social media were banned tomorrow, the shared resource would keep getting consumed — now by agents. There may be an "agentic commons" problem worth naming alongside the attention-economy one.
